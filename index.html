Summary for the owner

  • $1.1M is supported by several independent methods. Adjusted closed sales, a depreciated replacement-cost check, a heated-plus-kennel component method and a recent vet hospital sale all point to roughly $1.06M–$1.19M.
  • What changed from the July opinion. The July BPO applied comp prices only to the 5,647 SF heated area, which gave the 1,618 SF of kennel and storage space no value. The comps are measured on gross building area, so this update applies them to the subject's 7,265 SF gross area and deducts for the lower-quality kennel space.
  • Sale beats holding vacant. The building has been listed for lease since 12/1/2025 at $19.50/SF NNN without a signed lease. Holding vacant costs an estimated $20K–$27K a year. Leasing first could lift value to $1.16M–$1.22M, but only after 9–12+ months, about $90K–$110K in tenant improvements and commissions, carrying costs and lease-up risk. That leaves little or no net gain.
  • Area crime is down, not up. Reported incidents within half a mile fell about 43% from 2023 to 2026. The subject's area ranks mid-pack against the comp locations. There are some real recent concerns nearby, which are covered below.
  • Counter-evidence buyers will raise. Older, general-purpose buildings on Bragg Blvd have recently sold for only $52–$67/SF. The 2026 county tax value is $944,345. Both are addressed in the reconciliation.

Reconciliation

The most weight goes to the adjusted sales comparison ($1.09M–$1.19M), the component method ($1.10M) and the cost check ($1.11M–$1.18M). These methods reflect how an owner-user buyer, such as a veterinarian, dentist or medical practice, would price this building. The as-is income approach and the Riverbark sale support the lower half of the range.

Counter-evidence, disclosed: Three older, general-purpose buildings on the corridor sold for $52–$67/SF: 3116 Bragg (former restaurant, $300K, 4/2026), 4441 Bragg (office, $525K, 2/2025) and 5216 Bragg (office, $450K, 9/2025). Those buildings do not have medical build-out and some had deferred maintenance, so they are not given weight. Still, they show what a non-medical buyer may offer, which is why a medical or veterinary user is the target buyer.

What the data shows

  • The trend is improving. Reported incidents within half a mile fell from 435 in 2023 to 267 in 2025, and 2026 is on pace for about 250. The area's share of citywide crime dropped from 2.48% to about 1.74%.
  • It is still a busy commercial corridor. Incident density within half a mile is about 3.5 times the citywide average per square mile, which is typical along a major retail strip.
  • Most incidents are retail property crime. Since 1/2025, the most common incidents within a quarter mile were shoplifting (32), vandalism (21), other larceny (20) and simple assault (12). The convenience store at 3308 Bragg, across the street, accounts for 43 of the 169 incidents.
  • There are real recent concerns. Shootings into an occupied home or vehicle were reported on Chandler St behind the corridor on 5/29, 6/17 and 8/27/2026. Four business break-ins happened at 3430–3450 Bragg in 9/2025, and there have been several robberies nearby. Buyers will ask about these.
  • Compared with the comp locations. The subject's area (488 incidents since 1/2025) is mid-range. Three comps near the hospital district and downtown had more incidents (542–634) and still sold for $139–$181/SF.
  • Citywide context. Fayetteville's 2025 violent crime rate was 613.9 per 100,000 residents, compared with 330.6 statewide. In the first half of 2026, homicides were down 46% but crimes against persons were up 12.5%.
Recommendation: a vacant building on a busy corridor invites vandalism and trespass, so selling sooner limits that exposure. Until a sale closes, keep the building secured with lighting, cameras and regular checks, and let the insurer know it is vacant.
